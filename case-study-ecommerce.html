Sample figures

Why don't they just work harder? Because the first knot is arithmetic. Drag the sliders: on a normal Tuesday, a fraction of a serum a minute and a 25-minute update means a handful sold on an old number, which the stock absorbs. At 11.11 midnight, 12 a minute for 25 minutes is 300 serums sold on a number that is already wrong. No amount of care fixes that. Then three more knots pull at the same time: gift sets use components nobody deducts, marketplace money arrives net and a week or two later, and returns go back on the shelf unchecked. Each alone is manageable; on a mega-sale day they all tighten together. Sample figures throughout; payout timing and fees differ by marketplace and contract.

The answer is one database with one stock figure that every shop front reads. Click the four flows. Orders: website orders are Sales orders straight away; Shopee, Lazada and TikTok Shop orders come in through Odoo's connectors with their delivery slips, on each connector's sync schedule, not instantly. Stock: one on-hand figure per product, gift sets set up as kits so selling one takes the serum, toner and cream, and stock levels go back out to the marketplaces. Money: marketplace settlement reports imported into Accounting and matched to the bank, two slides on. Returns: a request in Helpdesk, a return receipt, a quality check, then restock or scrap. Honest notes: the three connectors are Odoo Enterprise features, documented for Singapore; Lazada and TikTok Shop are in the Odoo 20 release notes; exact sync behaviour and fulfilment types are confirmed per marketplace in discovery. Kits need the Manufacturing app for the bill of materials. Email Marketing reaches website customers who opted in; marketplace buyers' contact details stay with the marketplace.

Illustrative · sample data and sync times

Same night, same demand, played twice. Start on One Odoo stock and press Play or step through. Every order, from any shop, reserves from one forecasted figure, and a Glow Trio reserves a serum too, because the gift set is a kit. At 00:17 the forecast hits zero: the website stops selling at once, and the marketplaces get the new level at their next sync. Orders taken in that gap, 12 in this sample, show up in Sales at 00:20, so someone deals with 12 customers at twenty past midnight instead of 300 at 9 am. Now switch to By hand: the same demand, but the shops keep showing 600 until 00:55. Honest note: Odoo does not make a marketplace real-time; orders arrive and stock goes out on each connector's schedule, which we confirm per marketplace. For a mega-sale we also agree a small safety margin per shop.

Illustrative · sample fees, not any marketplace’s actual rates

This is the knot that hurts at month-end. Step through it. Each marketplace order is invoiced into a clearing account for that marketplace, so revenue is the full selling price, not whatever arrived. When the payout comes, the marketplace's settlement report is imported into Accounting, and commission, transaction fees, seller-funded vouchers, shipping and refunds land on their own expense lines. Then the bank line is fetched, matched, and the clearing account goes back to zero: every dollar sold is accounted for. Switch marketplaces: the fees and the timing differ, the method doesn't. Honest notes: the rates here are samples, not any marketplace's actual fees. Odoo's docs say fees and payouts are reconciled from the marketplace's financial reports imported into Accounting, not by the connector itself; the import is set up per marketplace. Bank sync depends on the bank being on Odoo's list; otherwise statements are imported. The GST treatment of fees is confirmed with the client's accountant.
