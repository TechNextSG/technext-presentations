This is the knot, and the room should feel it. Six orders for five customers, planned on the whiteboard; every job is laser, bend, weld, then out to the coater for a day. The flags are the promised dates. Everything is on time. Now press Break Bend 1: the main press brake stops on Tuesday at ten for two days. The whiteboard has no answer but to wait, so every job behind it slides right, and five orders for five customers go late, seventy hours in total. Press Steel sheet late too: two jobs wait for 2 mm sheet, and because the shop works the board in order, the laser waits with them. Nobody can see this on Monday; the customers find out on the promised day. Sample week: eight-hour days, a nine-day window.

Sample job and rates

Here is why it is structurally hard, and why a better spreadsheet doesn’t fix it. Take one finished job, J-2398, and open its real cost with the arrow key. Excel priced it by the kilogram plus a flat labour rate, with a 35% markup, so on paper it made about 26%. Then: six press-brake set-ups nobody quoted, welds that took eighteen minutes instead of twelve, twenty-four parts bent to the old revision because the floor had the Rev B print, offcuts and scrap, and an express coating run to rescue the date. The real cost ends above the price: about minus 4%. The point is not that someone made a mistake. The cost, the time and the revision live in three places that never meet, and nothing flows back to the next quote, so the shop quotes the same loss again.

Illustrative · sample data

Same six orders, same two problems, now in Odoo. Step through or press Play. One: the press-brake fault is a corrective maintenance request on the Bend 1 work centre with Block Workcenter ticked, so nothing more is scheduled on it. Two: Plan on the manufacturing orders routes the bending to the alternative work centre, the smaller Bend 2, which runs about 25% slower in the sample. Three: the steel supplier’s new arrival date is on the purchase order on Monday, so the two jobs waiting for it are planned after it and the laser cuts the other jobs first. That leaves one order late by about an hour. Four: sales sees it on the order and tells Customer D early in the week, not on the promised day. Five: preventive maintenance on Bend 1 goes into the maintenance calendar for a Saturday. Honest limits: Odoo schedules each work order into its work centre’s working hours, after what is already booked there, and per the docs Plan uses an alternative work centre when the first is unavailable or at capacity. It is not an optimiser that re-sequences the whole shop by profit; the planner still sets priorities and can drag work orders in the Gantt. We confirm the blocking behaviour on their database.

Illustrative · sample rates and part

This is the answer to the post-mortem. In Odoo the part has a bill of materials and a routing: each operation has minutes per piece on a work centre, and each work centre has a cost per hour and a set-up time. Odoo’s BoM overview costs the part from those, the same way it costs the manufacturing order, and the real cost of finished orders then shows where the estimate was wrong. Drag the quantity. Excel quotes S$20.09 a piece at any quantity, because it spreads labour flat. The real cost doesn’t: at 20 pieces the set-ups and the coater’s lot charge eat the margin and the job loses money; at 1,000 pieces the Excel price is higher than it needs to be, which is how the shop loses bids it should win. Drag scrap too. Honest notes: the minutes come from the routing, and they are only as good as the times the shop records on the Shop Floor tablets; scrap is set here as an allowance in the quote, and in production it is recorded as scrap orders on the manufacturing order.
