Each seam has an owner who patches it by hand. None of them can fix the next seam, because it lives in someone else’s system.One product, one customer, one stock figure and one ledger: the seams disappear because there is nothing to join.

Why don’t they just connect the systems? Because there are five seams, not one, and each is patched by a different person. Stock: the website reads a nightly file, so a store can sell what the web just promised. Customer: points in the store app and vouchers online. Price: an online promotion the store is then asked to match. Returns: online orders come back to stores, where the till can’t find them. Money: till reports, gateway payouts and marketplace statements in three places. Switch to One Odoo. Each seam closes for the same reason: the store and the website are two front doors on one database, so there is nothing to sync. That is the selling point of Odoo for this kind of business: Point of Sale, eCommerce, Inventory, Accounting and CRM are one system, not five integrations.

This is Odoo’s strongest argument for a business that is both retail and ecommerce: the five apps it needs are one product on one database. Click each. Point of Sale runs a till per store on the same product list and prices, and can load website orders for collection. eCommerce sells the stores’ stock, with click and collect from the store that has it. Inventory keeps one stock figure per product per place: the warehouse and each store. Accounting receives every till session, every online payment and every marketplace payout. CRM keeps the one customer, and turns a corporate enquiry into a pipeline. Sales, Purchase, Email Marketing and Dashboards sit on the same data. Honest notes: the Shopee and Lazada connectors are Odoo Enterprise features and sync on a schedule, not instantly; we confirm each marketplace in discovery.

Same Saturday, same shoppers, on one database. Step through. One: the product page shows each store’s stock, because Click and Collect with Show Available Qty reads the stores’ warehouses directly. Two: she chooses Katong and Pay on Site, which Odoo turns on with Click and Collect. The order reserves one set. Three: Katong’s staff get the pick and move the set from the floor to a collect shelf, so it can’t be sold again. Four: a walk-in buys the other set at the till; the website stops offering Katong and suggests Bishan, because a customer can’t pick a store that is out of stock. Five: at 17:30 the cashier opens Quotations/Orders in Point of Sale, loads S10482 and settles it; she pays, earns points on the same card, and the delivery is done. Honest notes: the two-step pick to a collect shelf is a route we configure per store; online pre-payment works too, and Pay on Site is the default Odoo switches on. We confirm the settle flow on the client’s Odoo version in discovery.

Step through the close of the same Saturday. One: each store closes its Point of Sale session, and closing posts that store’s sales and payments to Accounting by payment method: cash, card, PayNow. Two: website payments go through an online payment provider and are matched to its payout in Bank Matching. Three: Shopee and Lazada payouts are imported from their settlement reports into a clearing account per marketplace, with commission, fees and refunds on their own lines. Four: because all of it sits on the same product cost, Harrowmere can finally see what one dinner set keeps in each channel. Drag the marketplace fee: at 18 percent plus a seller voucher the marketplace sale keeps much less than click and collect, which is why stores that ship and collect matter. Every rate here is a sample, not any provider’s or marketplace’s actual fee; bank sync depends on the bank being on Odoo’s list, otherwise statements are imported; the GST treatment is confirmed with the client’s accountant.
