the request for proposal

Built to your brief, line by line.

Behind the design sits a working WordPress build, so we could prove each requirement before quoting it.

Requirement 2.1

Classic WordPress, as asked

header.php, index.php, single.php, page.php, footer.php, sidebar.php: the classic template files. No block theme, no theme.json, no full-site editing.

Your editors use the Classic Editor and Classic Widgets only. Gutenberg is switched off by the site's own plugin.

Requirement 2.2

A GeneratePress child theme

"HST Medical" runs as a child of GeneratePress, so the parent can update safely. Site facts live in Appearance › Customize, not in code.

Comes with an editor guide: where to change a price, the promotion band, the stores, the menus and the widgets.

Requirement 2.3

EasyCart, styled to fit

All 51 products in WP EasyCart, pack sizes as option items, Singapore checkout. Product, cart and checkout pages styled without breaking EasyCart's own layout.

EasyCart's 1.1 MB of scripts now load only on store screens, not on every page.

Requirement 2.4

Fast on a phone on 4G

One stylesheet, one small script, one self-hosted font, no jQuery outside the store, no carousels. Every image has its size, so nothing jumps.

Requirement 2.5

Readable by people and by AI

Clear H1–H4, comfortable line lengths, schema for products, prices and FAQs, a sitemap, and an llms.txt file that tells AI search what HST sells.

Requirement 2.6

Passes WordPress Theme Check

Vanilla WordPress hooks and template tags, GPL headers, no framework lock-in. Theme Check: 0 required findings.

The two remaining warnings sit in the GeneratePress parent's own files, not in ours.

Checked on our WordPress staging build, 3 October 2026

The RFP asks for a classic WordPress theme on GeneratePress with WP EasyCart, mobile-first and fast on 4G, typography-led with SEO and GEO, and WordPress.org standards.             The same three questions, asked of the rule-based assistant and of an AI assistant. The left column is the prototype's real answer today. The right column is illustrative: how a language-model assistant, restricted to your labels and with safety rules, would answer. First, a medicine question: rules find the catalogue caution but still show the product to buy; the AI recognises a question about mixing medicines and hands over to a pharmacist. Second, a question in Chinese: rules fall back to the help menu; the AI answers in Chinese from the English label. Third, context: rules list joint products by keyword; the AI picks the easiest format for an older user and says why, in the label's words. AI understands; the guardrails keep it on the label.
