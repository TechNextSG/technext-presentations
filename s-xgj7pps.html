Kinsail is a sample, but this shape is common: four GP clinics, two aesthetic clinics, and a central store that restocks them every week from WhatsApp lists. Click round the map. Every clinic keeps stock its own way, in Excel, a shared sheet or a paper card, and none of them records the lot number. Most GP visits are panel visits, billed to a company or a third-party administrator, and those claims are keyed in at month-end when someone has time.

The clinic system, the EMR, is fine. It holds the consultation. It just doesn’t hold the stock, and the stock sheets don’t hold the visit.

Sample recall · sample figures

Walk this with the arrow key and watch the clock. Monday, 9:10, a supplier recalls one vaccine lot. The central store knows it received 400 doses, but its receipt sheet has no lot column. So the clinics are phoned, and nurses read lot numbers off boxes in the fridge, between patients. Woodlands has a locum on Monday, so the answer comes Tuesday. The aesthetic clinics don’t carry flu vaccine, but nobody can prove that from the sheets, so they check too.

Then the hard question: who was given it? The clinic system records “influenza vaccine” on the visit, not the lot. So all 1,140 people vaccinated since the delivery have to be contacted. Press “What happened”: only 212 actually received this lot. That gap, 1,140 letters instead of 212, is the cost of a missing column. This is a sample scenario; recall steps follow the supplier’s and the authority’s instructions.

Illustrative · sample figures

This is why it is hard, and why a better spreadsheet doesn’t fix it. The stock sheet holds a date, an item and a quantity. Four columns are missing: the lot, the expiry, the visit it went to, and who pays for that visit. Those facts exist, but in three different places: the box, the clinic system and the panel portals.

Each missing pair is a bill. Lot and visit: recalls answered blind. Lot and expiry: stock found expired at the quarterly check and written off; the heat map is six clinics by six months. Visit and billed-to: panel visits wait for someone to key them in, so S$186,100 is more than 30 days old and not even invoiced yet. All sample figures. One root cause, three costs.

The answer starts from one record: the lot. In Odoo 20, with lots and expiration dates switched on, every vaccine and medicine lot has an expiration date, a removal date and an alert date, knows where its stock is, and has a Traceability report. Click each app and watch which part of the lot record it fills. Purchase writes the receipt, Inventory moves it to the clinics, Point of Sale and Sales record who it went to, Accounting prints the lot on the invoice and chases the panel.

Honest limits. Odoo is not an EMR or a clinic management system: consultations, diagnoses and prescriptions stay in the clinic’s system, and we integrate where it helps. Patient contact details in Odoo are personal data under the PDPA, so we keep only what billing and recalls need, with access rights per role. Sign and Documents are for consent forms and supplier paperwork, not clinical notes.

Same recall, with Odoo. Step through it, or pick another lot at the top to show it isn’t staged. Search the lot. The Traceability report shows where it came from: the vendor receipt at the central store, with the lot and expiry entered once. Then the four internal transfers to the clinics. Then every sale: self-pay sales at the Point of Sale with the customer on the order, and panel visits as sales orders whose invoice prints the lot. Step five: what is left moves to a Quarantine location so FEFO can never pick it, and the customers on those sales are the contact list: 212, not 1,140.

Honest limits: patient-level tracing works through the sales of the lot, so it depends on the lot and the customer or visit reference being on every sale. Odoo does not read the clinic’s medical record. For panel visits, the patient is identified by the visit reference that links back to the clinic system.

Illustrative · sample panels and amounts

The second knot is money. Before, panel visits wait for someone to key them into each panel’s portal at month-end, so S$278,400 sits unbilled, more than half of it older than 30 days. Columns are days since the visit. Step through. One: every Friday, accounts open Sales, Orders to Invoice, select the panel orders and Create Invoices with Consolidated Billing: one invoice per panel, each line with its visit reference. Two: Follow-up Reports send reminders by level, so the oldest are chased first. Three: payments come in and Bank Matching reconciles them, and the board drains. What is left in 90+ is in dispute, and now you know exactly which visits.

Honest limits: some TPAs require their own portal or file format. Odoo produces the invoice and the statement; the portal upload is by export or a connector we confirm per TPA. Panel prices and co-payments come from each panel contract, set up as pricelists. All panels and amounts are samples.
