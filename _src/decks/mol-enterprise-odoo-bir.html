The rules in plain words. RR 26-2025 sets the date: covered taxpayers issue compliant e-invoices on or before 31 December 2026. RMC 98-2026, issued 22 September 2026, sets the working rules: a Permit to Issue (PTI) before the first e-invoice, and EIS Certification within six months of the PTI. An issued e-invoice can’t be deleted or changed: a credit note lowers an amount, a new e-invoice raises it. If the system is down, a BIR-authorised manual invoice is issued, then an e-invoice that refers to it. The system sends the required sales data to the BIR’s EIS. Click any dot on the line for its rule; the pin shows today. The counter uses today’s date. Note how close this sits to the Autocount expiry: both fall in December 2026. Who is covered: sellers through e-commerce or internet transactions, Large Taxpayers Service, large taxpayers (RA 11976, RR 8-2024), and CAS or CBA users with invoicing software; micro taxpayers are exempt; head office and branches are covered. Click a group to mark it for the accountant to check: we do not decide which group MOL Enterprise is in, their accountant or RDO confirms. General guidance, not tax advice. Source: technext.asia/bir-e-invoicing.

Two things on one slide. Left: the working days left to 31 December, Monday to Friday with Philippine holidays taken out (Proclamation No. 1006), counted live from today, plus the clock to 11:59 pm Manila time on the 31st; click an amber day to name the holiday. This is the time we actually have to set things up with them. Right: the penalties the Tax Code already has, which RR 11-2025 points e-invoicing violations to. Section 264(a): failing or refusing to issue an invoice, a fine of 1,000 to 50,000 pesos and 2 to 4 years. Section 264-A: failing to transmit sales data, 10,000 pesos a day or one-tenth of one percent of annual net income a day, whichever is higher, and permanent closure after 180 days in a year; say clearly that this applies once the BIR switches on electronic sales reporting, which is a separate step it has not started. The BIR has not published a penalty for the 31 December date itself and the Commissioner can still extend it. We are not the BIR and do not decide penalties; their accountant or RDO confirms what applies. No scare talk: the point is to start early.
