The rules in plain words. Click the dots on the line. RR 26-2025 set the deadline: covered taxpayers issue compliant e-invoices on or before 31 December 2026. RMC 98-2026, issued 22 September 2026, sets the working rules: a Permit to Issue (PTI) before the first e-invoice, and EIS Certification within six months of the PTI. Four rules matter for day-to-day work: an issued e-invoice can't be deleted or changed; a credit note lowers an amount and a new e-invoice raises it; the system sends the required sales data to the BIR's EIS; and when the system is down, a BIR-authorised manual invoice is issued, then replaced by an e-invoice that refers to it. The day counter uses today's date. Source: technext.asia/bir-e-invoicing, which cites RR 26-2025, RMC 98-2026 and the BIR advisory of 14 Sep 2026. We are not the BIR; their accountant or RDO has the final word.

{{icon:file}}Groups as listed under RR 26-2025 and RMC 98-2026 · the Commissioner may add others

Who the rules cover, as listed: small, medium and large taxpayers selling through e-commerce or internet transactions; taxpayers under the Large Taxpayers Service; large taxpayers under RA 11976 and RR 8-2024; and businesses on a CAS or CBA with e-invoicing or other invoicing software. Micro taxpayers are exempt, and the Commissioner can add others. Cotton Central sells online, so the e-commerce group is the one to check, but we do not decide it: the accountant or RDO confirms. If they turn out to be micro, the deadline does not apply, and Odoo still helps with clean invoicing; say that honestly.
