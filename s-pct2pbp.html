Sample figures · one project replayed

This is the heart of it. P02: a level 18 office, contract sum S$1.86 million, budgeted cost S$1.58 million, so about 15 percent planned margin. The blue line is the cost the monthly report saw. It ran just under budget every month, so the report said 17.6 percent all the way. Step through the nine months, then switch on the three things the report could not see. Variations the client asked for on site and the supervisor agreed on WhatsApp: the work was done and the cost was real, but it was never priced or claimed. Materials bought for this job but booked to others. And subcontractor work done but not yet invoiced, some of it still outstanding at handover. Each one is small in a month. Together they turn a 17.6 percent job into a loss of S$48,600 at the final account. The retention line is cash, not cost: 5 percent of every claim, S$93,000 by the end, held by the main contractor, and nobody owns the date to claim it back. Every number is sample data.

Illustrative · sample month

Why it is structurally hard, and why a better spreadsheet doesn’t fix it. A contractor runs on three clocks. The site moves every day, and decisions are made on the spot. Subcontractors invoice when they get round to it, often a month or more after the work. And the office cuts the progress claim on a fixed date, the 25th here. Step through month six of P02. On day 3 the client’s project manager asks for fourteen extra power points and the supervisor agrees on WhatsApp. On day 9 twelve gypsum boards go to another site, still booked to P02. On day 12 the electrician invoices last month’s work; the air-conditioning sub hasn’t invoiced since March. On day 22 the QS rings the supervisor for percent complete. On the 25th the claim goes out built from those guesses, without the variations. On the 30th the cost report closes and says 17.6 percent. The panel on the right is what Claim 06 really contained.

Illustrative screen · sample data

The answer is not a construction add-on. It is standard Odoo 20 apps that all post to one analytic account per project, so the project’s Profitability panel shows revenue and cost as they happen. Switch the apps on one by one and watch the margin: with only Sales on, P02 looks wildly profitable, which is exactly the trap of the old monthly report. Purchase is the big one: a subcontract purchase order counts as cost in the panel from the day it is confirmed, so the S$104,000 the subcontractors haven’t billed yet is already in the number. Analytic distribution is made mandatory on purchase and bill lines, so nothing is bought without a project. With all five on, P02 shows about 16 percent at month seven: a true number, early enough to act on. Honest notes: Odoo has no built-in retention feature; we set it up as a retention line on the claim posted to a retention receivable account, or as an instalment in the payment terms. Odoo is not a BIM or CAD tool; drawings live in Documents as files.

Press Play or step through it. This is the variation that leaked on slide three, done the Odoo way. The supervisor logs VO-07 as a task in the P02 project from Odoo on his phone, with before photos. The project manager prices it as a quotation linked to the same project and sends it; the client signs it online, which Sales does as standard, so there is a signed record before the work starts. Illustrative screen · sample contract

This replaces the Excel claim. The contract is a sales order with one line per trade, each a service invoiced on delivered quantity, so “delivered” is simply the percent complete, which the QS sets from the supervisors’ task updates, not from a phone call. Drag a line: Odoo invoices only the difference between what is delivered to date and what was already invoiced, so previous claims are deducted by design. The signed variation sits on the same order. Press Create Invoice: the draft invoice carries this claim’s lines and a retention line. Honest limits, say them plainly: Odoo has no built-in retention feature, so retention is a line and an account we configure, and its release is a separate invoice when the defects period ends. A claim document in the client’s usual format, with previous and cumulative columns, is a report layout we set up. Odoo also offers invoicing based on project milestones, where reaching a milestone bills a set percent; we choose per contract. GST is added on the real invoice; the sample stops before GST.
