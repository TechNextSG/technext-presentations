The real problemisn’t the price rise. It’s that the business found out five weeks later, and still can’t say which dish caused it.

Sample figures

Walk this with the arrow key: it is the story every F&B owner recognises. One ingredient goes up. The supplier tells purchasing, purchasing pays, and nothing else in the business hears about it. The recipe cost is in an Excel file no one opens, the menu price is spread across three POS brands, and the delivery apps take their cut of a margin that has already shrunk. Only at month-end does the accountant see food cost go from 30 to 33 percent, and by then nobody can say which dish did it. The sample figures: 9,000 chicken rice sets a month across five outlets, S$0.21 more per set, about S$1,890 a month.

Illustrative · sample data

Press Play or step through it. Laksa paste at Outlet 3 drops below its minimum of 8 kg. A reordering rule creates a resupply transfer from the central kitchen. The kitchen doesn’t have enough paste, so a manufacturing order is planned from the bill of materials, and because one ingredient is short, a request for quotation is drafted to the preferred supplier for the buyer to approve. The batch is cooked, gets a quality check and a lot with an expiry date, and the outlet scans it in the next morning. Every document is created from the one before. Odoo features used: reordering rules, multi-warehouse resupply, manufacturing from a bill of materials, lots and expiration dates, quality control points.

Illustrative · sample prices and commission

This is the slide that answers the price-rise story. The chicken rice set has a bill of materials, so its cost is known to the cent, and the product cost can be computed from it. Drag the chicken price: at S$7.20 the food cost is about 31%, on target. Push it past about S$7.70 and it crosses the 32% target, flagged the same day rather than at month-end. Switch to the delivery app: the sample uses a S$9.40 delivery price, a 30% platform commission and takeaway packaging, and the margin is very different. Underneath, the flow: the order is taken at the table, goes to the Kitchen Display, the sale deducts the components from stock (a kit bill of materials), and the dashboard shows food cost by dish. Commission rates differ by platform and contract: these are sample numbers.
