Sample member and figures

Walk it with the arrow key. One member buys a Reformer 10-pack for S$320. The bookkeeper sees S$320 arrive and posts it as March sales: on the books it is all earned on day one. In truth it is earned S$32 at a time: three classes in March, two and a no-show in April, two in June. The no-show credit is taken under the studio’s policy, and the member argues about it. In September the pack expires with two credits left. The booking app quietly zeroes them, the member complains, and a manager promises an extension on WhatsApp that is recorded nowhere. At month-end nobody can say whether that S$64 is owed or earned. Multiply by 750 open packs and the sample says about S$186,240 is owed to members while the books call it sales. This is not a clinical or health matter: it is bookings and money.

The root: money, bookings, staff and rooms each live in a different system, so no one record knows what a member paid, used and is still owed.

Press the arrow four times. Leak one is the deepest: a credit has no single price. An intro credit is worth S$15, a 10-pack credit S$32, a 20-pack credit S$28, and a free credit nothing, so the booking app’s 7,420 open credits cannot simply be turned into dollars. Leak two: on the 1st, 1,100 cards are charged; 66 are declined, the app keeps those members booking for 30 days, and 26 never pay. Leak three: instructors are paid from paper sign-in sheets, which never match the app. Leak four: the spa sells the same room from two diaries. The sample adds up to about S$8,554 a month, plus the S$412,640 that no report shows. All four have one root: no single member record.

Illustrative · simplified Odoo screen, sample member

The answer is one record per member, with every app hanging off it. Click the smart buttons: the membership is a subscription with a saved card; the pack is an eWallet balance in dollars; bookings are Appointments; packs and mats sold online are eCommerce orders; check-ins and retail are Point of Sale orders; and Accounting holds the invoices. Behind the desk: Planning for instructors and therapists, Marketing Automation for reminders, Accounting for what is owed. Honest notes for the room: Odoo has no object called a class credit. We model packs as eWallet balances in dollars, which carry an expiry date and are redeemed at check-in. If a client wants a credit taken automatically at the moment of booking, that is a customisation we scope. And on failed renewals, Odoo tags the subscription Payment Failure and holds it; there is no multi-step retry schedule like a card-billing service’s dunning, so the front desk works the list, and the plan’s Automatic Closing closes it if it stays unpaid.

Illustrative · sample references and account

Same member, same pack, now in Odoo. Step through it. The pack is sold on the website shop as an eWallet top-up of S$320, and the member’s eWallet carries an expiry date. Because the top-up product posts to a liability account, the S$320 lands as owed, not as sales. She books a Reformer class through Appointments: the studio room is a resource with Manage Capacities, so the class stops at 12 places. At check-in the front desk takes S$32 from her eWallet on the Point of Sale: the liability falls and revenue rises by the same amount, class by class. When the pack expires with S$64 left, the accountant posts one entry to settle it, by the studio’s own policy. Honest limits: the expiry settlement is an entry the accountant posts, not automatic; Odoo’s deferred revenue spreads by date, so we use it for annual memberships paid up front, not for packs, which are used unevenly; and how expired balances are treated is for the client’s accountant to decide.

Illustrative · sample rooms and bookings

The spa’s double bookings came from two diaries selling one room. In Odoo each treatment room is a resource in Appointments, and each treatment is an appointment type that uses only the rooms set up for it: massage in rooms 1 to 3, facials in rooms 3 and 4. Click a booked hour: it is refused, and the website never offered it. Click a free one: it is booked, and the online list updates at once. Switch to Facial to see rooms 1 and 2 drop out. The strip underneath is the therapists on shift, from Planning. Honest limit: a standard appointment type is scheduled on rooms or on staff, not both at once, so here the room is the hard check and the front desk matches the therapist from Planning. Holding a room and a named therapist together in one online booking is a customisation we would scope.
