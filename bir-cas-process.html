what it is · why now

Register the system first, go live second.

A Computerized Accounting System (CAS) replaces manual and loose-leaf books. The BIR registers your use of it before it keeps your books or issues your invoices.

Who registers
Businesses that keep books or issue invoices through software
What you get
An Acknowledgement Certificate (AC) with its control number
Why now
No demo is needed first, and the Ease of Paying Taxes (EOPT) invoice rules count as a major change: a system registered before must update its registration
  1. ApplyOnline (ORUS) or at your RDO
  2. AC issuedThe BIR checks completeness
  3. Go liveBooks and invoices from the system
  4. Post-evaluationLater, on the BIR's timing
Everything issued here is unregistered

Your books and invoices count from day one.No system demo is needed before go-live. The BIR reviews the system afterwards, so it must match what you registered from the first invoice.

Invoices from an unregistered system can draw Sec. 264 penalties.Books kept on it may also fail the Sec. 232 bookkeeping rules. The next slide shows what that costs.

CAS registration is the BIR's way of checking that software-generated books and invoices follow the Tax Code before a business relies on them. Rule of thumb: register first, go live second.

Press → (or the switch) to show the other order. The PDF and the overview show the recommended order, register first. Going live first means every invoice and book entry made before the Acknowledgement Certificate comes from an unregistered system.

Since RMC 5-2021 the BIR no longer asks for a system demonstration before go-live; it post-evaluates the system afterwards, on its own timing. That is a reason to be ready from the first invoice, not a reason to wait. We do not promise how long the BIR takes to issue the AC: that is the BIR's call.

Full legal basis: the Tax Code (NIRC); RR 9-2009; RMC 5-2021 and RMO 9-2021 (the operative CAS rules); RMC 3-2023 (e-books); RR 7-2024 (EOPT invoicing: the new invoice requirements count as a major enhancement, so an existing registration must be updated); RMO 7-2015 (compromise penalties); RA 10963 (TRAIN) and RA 11976 (EOPT). The AC has no expiry date; it stays valid unless revoked. It is not a permit to issue e-invoices (RMC 98-2026).

Any of these reviews can surface an unregistered system: the RDO's post-evaluation, an audit under a Letter of Authority, or a compliance verification drive. RMC 5-2021 says violations found at post-evaluation or audit are penalised under RMO 7-2015.

Click a row (or press →) to build its staircase: for the Sec. 264 and 266 rows the first and second offences can be settled by compromise; from the third offence there is no compromise and the case can go to court. The criminal line is the Tax Code section's own range: Sec. 264(a) is ₱1,000 to ₱50,000 and 2 to 4 years (Annex A misprints it; TRAIN left it unchanged).

If asked about corporations: the compromise is ₱10,000 or the scheduled amount, whichever is higher, plus ₱5,000 for each responsible officer (Sec. 256). Tax evasion (Sec. 254, as amended by TRAIN) is ₱500,000 to ₱10 million and 6 to 10 years, and is never compromisable.

Compromise amounts are from the BIR's Revised Schedule of Compromise Penalties, RMO 7-2015 Annex A. Deficiency tax, surcharge and interest are charged on top. Confirm current figures with your accountant or RDO before quoting them to a client.

Your steps sit in the top lane, the BIR's in the bottom lane. Press → to walk them, or click a step.

1 Prepare: the pack on the next slide. 2 Apply online in ORUS, or at your RDO or Large Taxpayers office: your business files it, under your TIN. 3 The RDO (or the Large Taxpayers office) checks it for completeness; if something is missing you fix the gap and resubmit. 4 The BIR issues the Acknowledgement Certificate with its control number. RMC 5-2021 sets three working days once the documents are complete, but the timing is the BIR's: never promise a date. 5 Go live: from then on the system is your official books, and the AC control number prints on system-generated invoices. 6 Register your e-books (a soft copy of the books, on USB, with a transmittal letter) in ORUS within 30 days after every taxable year (RMO 9-2021, RMC 3-2023). 7 The BIR post-evaluates the system against its standards whenever it chooses, and it can be checked again in any audit.

after go-live

Registered once, kept every year.

Registration is not the end: these obligations continue for as long as the system is in use. Press → to walk the year.

From day one: the registered system is your official books; the AC control number prints on every system-generated invoice; keep it configured as registered. Every day: retain and back up records, generally for 5 years under the EOPT Act (RA 11976, Sec. 33). Before a change: minor updates need a notice; a major enhancement needs an updated registration before you use it. Use a major upgrade before updating the registration and the registration has no effect from that date, and penalties may also apply (RMC 5-2021). The EOPT invoice changes (RR 7-2024) count as a major enhancement. Anytime: the RDO can post-evaluate the system; violations draw RMO 7-2015 penalties. After every taxable year: register your e-books (a soft copy on USB, with a transmittal letter) in ORUS within 30 days (RMO 9-2021, RMC 3-2023).

Odoo exports the books for the annual registration; the registration itself is yours.
